01 / EARN MORE

Your skills can become your first source of income.

You can start small. Focus on learning a useful skill and offering real value.

01

Tutoring

Teach subjects you understand well to school students or juniors.

First step: Choose one subject and prepare a short sample lesson.

02

Freelancing

Offer services such as basic graphic design, writing, video editing or web development.

First step: Make two sample projects to show your skills.

03

Campus opportunities

Look for legitimate paid events, student roles, internships or college opportunities.

First step: Check official college notices and placement channels.

04

Sell a useful service

Help people with a skill you already have, such as making presentations or editing documents.

First step: Ask people what small task they need help with.

Be careful of jobs that ask you to pay registration fees, share OTPs or promise guaranteed high income.

02 / SPEND SMARTER

Pause before you pay.

A good spending decision considers what you need now and what you may need later.

Need or want?

NEED

Essential expenses

Food, transport, study materials and necessary bills.

WANT

Optional expenses

Extra shopping, entertainment and impulse purchases.

Before buying, ask yourself:

  • Do I really need this right now?
  • Will I still have enough for essentials?
  • Can I find a less expensive option?
  • Will I regret spending this money tomorrow?
03 / SAVE BETTER

Saving is a habit, not a competition.

You do not need to save a huge amount. Start with an amount that your situation can actually support.

A SIMPLE EXAMPLE

What can a small habit do?

If you choose to save ₹20 each day:

₹600 in 30 days

This is only an example. Save only after accounting for your essential expenses and available money.

Build an emergency buffer

Keep some money aside, if possible, for unexpected needs such as travel or study expenses.

Make saving realistic

Choose a manageable amount. If your income is limited or zero, do not force a saving target that you cannot afford.

04 / MONEY BASICS

Understand the language of money.

These basic terms help you understand your own financial situation.

Income

Money you receive, such as allowance, salary or earnings.

Expense

Money you spend on goods, services or bills.

Budget

A plan for how you will use the money available to you.

Savings

Money you choose to keep instead of spending now.

Interest

Money paid for borrowing, or earned on certain savings products.

Inflation

A general rise in prices that reduces what the same amount of money can buy.

05 / BANKING FOR STUDENTS

Get comfortable with everyday banking.

Knowing the basics can help you use banking services more confidently and safely.

What is a bank account?

A bank account is a place where a bank records and holds your money. It can help you receive money, make payments and keep track of transactions.

What is a savings account?

A savings account is a common type of bank account used to deposit money and access it when needed. Features, fees and interest rates vary by bank and account.

What is a debit card?

A debit card lets you make payments or withdraw cash using money available in your linked bank account, subject to your bank's rules.

What is UPI?

UPI is a system that allows people to send and receive money digitally between participating bank accounts.

What may I need to open an account?

Requirements vary by bank and account type. Banks commonly ask for identity and address verification, photographs or other details. Check the bank's official website or branch for the current list.

BANKING SAFETY

Protect your money and your details.

  • Never share your UPI PIN, OTP or card PIN.
  • Do not click unknown payment links.
  • Check the receiver's name before paying.
  • Use official bank apps and websites.
  • Contact your bank immediately if you notice an unauthorised transaction.
